## 1. Market size

### 1.1 National picture (the only rigorously sourced numbers)

**Source:** Colliers India press release, 8 May 2025 `[VERIFIED]`

| Metric | 2020 | 2025E | 2027F | 2030F |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demand (million beds) | 5.8 | 6.6 | 7.4 | 9.1 |
| Organised stock (million beds) | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.6 | 1.0 |
| **Penetration** | 3% | **5%** | 8% | 11% |
| Market size (₹ billion) | 22 | **40** | 100 | **206** |

**Headline interpretation:** the organised sector serves **under 5% of demand**. That is the
single most important structural fact about this market — it is not a competitive market,
it is an unconverted one.

**Supporting national figures** `[VERIFIED — Colliers, May 2025]`:
- Leading operators raised **~USD 1 billion since 2015**
- Co-living returns **~10%** vs **2–5%** for traditional residential rent
- Student demand **~12 million** vs campus accommodation capacity **~4 million** (AISHE FY2021-22)
  → an **~8 million bed gap**
- India higher-education: **43.3 million enrolled**, GER **28.4%**
- Urban migrants aged 20–34: **~50 million** (2025)

**Independent corroboration** `[SNIPPET — NOESIS via ET Travel, 2025]`: demand 6.6M beds,
organised supply <300,000 beds, <5% penetration. NOESIS is a commercial advisory firm, not
peer-reviewed.

**Market-size estimates vary by firm** `[ESTIMATE]`:

| Source | Figure | Basis |

**Operational footprint** `[ESTIMATE — Everything Coliving, 2026]`:

### 1.3 Fragmentation — hard evidence

Fragmentation is not just a market-structure observation; it is the mechanism producing
consumer harm. Three independent enforcement data points:

**GHMC Special Task Force inspection** — `[VERIFIED — NewsMeter, 24 May 2025]`
- **58 hostels** inspected in just three localities (Ameerpet, Ashok Nagar, Dilsukhnagar)
- **30 notices** issued · **5 kitchens sealed** · **₹2,45,500** in penalties under the GHMC Act, 1955

**Hyderabad City Police "Project Safe Stay"** — `[VERIFIED — NewsMeter, 18 Jul 2026]`
- **447 women's hostels** audited in the Hyderabad Commissionerate alone
- 10 inspection teams over 9 weekends

**Compliance rates from the same audit** — `[VERIFIED — Hyderabad Stories, 20 Jul 2026]`
- Trade-licence compliance: **44%**
- Overall score below 40/100: **62%**

| IT/ITES companies in Hyderabad | **1,500+** |
| GCCs | **~400**, employing **>3 lakh** professionals |
| Hyderabad share of India's GCCs | **~20%** |
| New GCCs added by Oct 2025 | **40** — highest in India |
| Services share of state GSVA (2025-26) | **68.6%** |
| Hyderabad share of India's GenAI startups | **~7%** (4th, behind Karnataka 39%, Maharashtra 14%, Delhi 9%) |

⚠️ **CONTRADICTION — the trajectory.** Deccan Herald (21 Mar 2026) reports **9.39 lakh** for
2024-25 and quotes opposition leader KTR stating jobs **fell** from 9.46 lakh (2023) to 9.39 lakh.
Hyderabad Mail (22 May 2026) `[VERIFIED]` reports 9.39 lakh in **March 2025** and states the
workforce had risen to **10.2 lakh by end-2025**, heading to 11 lakh by Dec 2026.

**Both cite government sources. They cannot both be right.** This matters enormously — a flat or
falling workforce means a renter's market with rising vacancy; a growing one means rent inflation.
**Resolve this before any demand model.**

**Also relevant** `[VERIFIED — Hyderabad Mail, 22 May 2026]`: Hyderabad office rentals are
**25–30% lower** than Bengaluru and Mumbai — a structural attraction for employer relocation.

### 3.4 Two structural findings worth designing around

**Finding 1 — Deposit norms are wildly inconsistent.** Same micro-market, same day:

| Rent | Deposit | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| ₹7,000 | ₹2,000 | 0.29× |
| ₹7,500 | ₹10,500 | **1.40×** |
| ₹5,000 | ₹10,000 | **2.00×** |
| ₹17,000 | ₹17,000 | 1.00× |

**No standard exists.** Most commonly ₹3,000–₹5,000 in the Financial District micro-market.
⚠️ No authoritative "typical deposit" statistic exists anywhere. `[UNVERIFIED]`

**Finding 2 — Curfews are a *published product attribute*.** Platforms expose a
**"Gate Closing Time"** field, observed at **11:36 PM** and **12:32 AM**. Restrictions on
tenants' movement aren't a hidden complaint — they are a marketed norm. This is important:
the market currently treats restricted freedom as a feature, not a defect.
