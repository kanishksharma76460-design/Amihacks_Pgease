## 1. Tourist arrivals

### 1.1 State-level, 2023–2026

**Source:** New Indian Express, 3 Sep 2026, citing a Telangana Tourism Ministry progress report
`[VERIFIED]`

| Year | Domestic | Foreign |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 5.84 crore | 1.60 lakh |
| 2024 | **8.82 crore** | 1.55 lakh |
| 2025 | **8.48 crore** | 1.71 lakh |
| Jan–Aug 2026 | 7.71 crore | 1.24 lakh |

**Three things to note:**

1. **Domestic visits declined 3.9% from 2024 to 2025.** The government's own progress report
   does not explain this reversal.
2. Foreign arrivals are **tiny** — ~1.6–1.7 lakh/year against ~8.5 crore domestic.
   Foreign tourists are **~0.2%** of volume.
3. Policy target is **5 lakh+ international tourists by 2030** — roughly **3x current volume**.

⚠️ **Fiscal-year vs calendar-year is not consistently clarified by the state.** A headline from
Andhra Jyothy attributes "8.82 crore" to **FY2024-25** while New Indian Express attributes the same
number to **CY2024** `[SNIPPET]`. **Do not mix these series.**

### 1.2 Where foreign tourists actually go

**Source:** The Hans India, 4 Dec 2025 `[VERIFIED]`

- 2024 (full year): **1,55,313** foreign tourists
- 2025 (1 Jan – 30 Nov): **1,60,988**; of these **1,59,203 (≈98.9%)** visited Hyderabad and
  nearby districts (Rangareddy, Medchal)
- After Hyderabad: Mulugu, Warangal Rural, Bhadradri Kothagudem
- Telangana **did not feature in the top 10 states** for foreign tourist arrivals

⚠️ **Industry disputes this.** IATO (AP & Telangana chapter), chairman K Ranga Reddy:
- Foreign tourist numbers have **fallen ~70%**
- Official data is **inflated** because IT companies' foreign executives on 1–7 week training
  visits have their attraction visits counted as tourist footfall
- "The reliable data comes from the Government of India"

### 1.3 Ministry of Tourism's independent view

**Source:** The Hindu, 25 Feb 2025, citing *India Tourism Data Compendium 2024* `[VERIFIED]`

- Telangana was top-10 for domestic tourist visits in **2013, 2019, 2022 — but dropped off in 2023**
- Telangana did **not** appear in the top 10 for foreign tourist visits in **any** of the four
  years examined

**This corroborates the decline, not the growth story.**

## 2. Economic contribution & policy

### 2.1 Telangana Tourism Policy 2025-2030

**Sources:** CSIS Engaging Indian States, 26 Mar 2025 `[VERIFIED]`; New Indian Express, 3 Sep 2026 `[VERIFIED]`

The **first comprehensive tourism policy since Telangana's formation in 2014.** In effect ~Mar 2025.

**Targets:**

| Target | Value |
|---|---|
| New tourism investment | **₹15,000 crore (US$1.74bn)** over 5 years |
| Additional jobs | **3 lakh** |
| Tourism share of state GDP | **≥10%** |
| International tourists by 2030 | **5 lakh+** |
| Domestic + international ranking | Top-5 state |

**Structure:** 27 **Special Tourism Areas** · 6 clusters (spiritual, heritage, eco-wellness,
handicraft, water, Buddhist) · Medical Enclaves & Wellness Zones · GST input subsidies ·
single-window clearance via **TG-iPASS** · PPP with 33–99 year allotments

### 2.3 Budget — the reveal

**Sources:** The Hindu / Financial Express / New Indian Express, March 2026 `[SNIPPET]`

## 7. Medical tourism

### 7.1 Market value

**Source:** Medical Buyer, 17 Mar 2026, reproducing The South First `[VERIFIED]`
- Hyderabad medical tourism market: **₹500 crore** (per Telangana government data)
- Projected to **triple to ₹1,500 crore within 3–4 years**
- India total medical value travel: **USD 20.4bn by 2026** → **USD 25–28bn by 2030** `[ESTIMATE]`

### 7.2 Patient volume

- **75,171 (2014) → 1,55,313 (2024)** foreign patients — "more than doubled"
- `[VERIFIED — Medical Buyer; corroborated by The Hindu]`
- **~1,000 international visitors per month** seeking medical care `[VERIFIED — Hotelivate, Apr 2025]`
- Source markets: **Somalia, Kenya, Sudan, Ethiopia, Zimbabwe, Nigeria, Senegal**; also Middle East
  and Southeast Asia

⚠️ **CRITICAL DATA-INTEGRITY ISSUE:** the figure **1,55,313** is used **both** as Telangana's
**total foreign tourist arrivals** in 2024 (The Hans India) **and** as Hyderabad's **foreign
patients** in 2024. A city cannot be 100% of a state's foreign arrivals *and* have that total
consist entirely of medical patients. **Most likely the "foreign patients" series is actually
the state's total foreign arrivals, relabelled.** Do not double-count. Flag prominently.

### 7.3 Policy development

- **Telangana State Medical Tourism Society (TGMTS)** proposed — policy round-table held
  **20 Feb 2026**. Role: single coordinating platform for global branding, international outreach,
  grievance redressal, and **licensing of facilitators** `[VERIFIED]`
- **Union Budget 2026 proposed five national MVT hubs** under 'Heal in India'
- Medical tourism hub embedded in the **'Telangana Rising 2047'** vision
- **Telemedicine / digital health** in the Tourism Policy's Medical Enclaves framework

### 7.4 Disruption

**Iran–Israel war (early 2026)** `[VERIFIED — Medical Buyer, 17 Mar 2026]`:
- ~**30% of expected March elective cases cancelled**
- Middle East patient flow dropped from **7–14/week → 1–2/week**
- Ethiopian Airlines fares rose from **~USD 1,000 → ~USD 2,500**
- Doctor outreach trips to Africa halted

## 8. Seasonality

### 8.1 Peak season: November – mid-February

**Source:** Deccan Chronicle, 1 Feb 2026 `[VERIFIED]`

- "Hyderabad enjoys a pleasant climate **from November to mid-February**, a period that attracts
  a large number of outstation and foreign tourists"
- Hotspots: Charminar, Golconda Fort, Birla Mandir, Hussain Sagar area, Nehru Zoological Park
- **Nehru Zoo: highest-ever single-day footfall 25,900 on 1 January**
- **Salar Jung Museum: >7,000/day in peak winter** (vs 3,000–4,000 usual)
- **Pearl sales nearly double** during these three months (Lad Bazaar)
- School/college study tours peak — "December and January are the best months to visit"
- Tourist vehicle demand: buses, SUVs, tempo travellers, Innovas "in constant demand every day"

**Off-season:** April–June touches **42°C** and hotels go cheap `[SNIPPET]`

## 9. Tourism pain points

### 9.1 Airport touting — the best-quantified problem

**Source:** Deccan Chronicle, 9 Jan 2025 `[VERIFIED]`

- **446 touting cases reported in 2024** to RGIA Police Station and Interception teams
- **31 anti-touting security personnel** deployed; special patrol with Telangana Police
- Unauthorised cabs charging **~₹800/head**, packing 3–4 passengers per cab, then
  **demanding ₹400 extra** at drop-off
- Documented route inflation: a 40-minute journey took **1 hour 20 minutes**
- "Touts are said to operate openly inside the airport premises" at night
- Complaints of a **"local mafia"** blocking Ola/Uber pickups
- **Quoted passenger:** "Getting Ola Cabs or Uber at Hyderabad Airport is a nightmare at night."

**Corroborating** `[VERIFIED — Newsmeter, 15 Jun 2024]`: brokers aggressively approach arriving
passengers; illegal taxis prevalent around **Uppal, LB Nagar, Aramgarh Crossroads, BHEL Crossroad**
using private white-plate vehicles. Telangana Gig and Platform Union president Shaik Salauddin:
unauthorised cabs "are a **security threat to the passengers**."

**Also:** ₹6,000 demanded for an airport cab ride `[SNIPPET — NDTV Profit, NewsBytes]`

### 9.2 Auto-rickshaw refusal and overcharging `[SNIPPET]`

- Driver double-booked rides, lied about routes, then **followed a woman demanding extra money** —
  triggered a wave of similar complaints (Asianet Newsable)
- "Locals tired of auto drivers refusing rides in Hyderabad" (Deccan Chronicle)
- Overcharging concerns during TGSRTC strike (Telangana Today)
- TGSRTC bus capacity strain, women pay the price (Siasat)

### 9.3 Fragmented information — admitted on the record

**Source:** The Hans India, 4 Dec 2025 `[VERIFIED]` — this is the most quotable evidence for the
information-gap thesis, because it is an industry insider admitting it:

> Travel agents state the government **spends lakhs promoting tourism in Italy, Germany and
> elsewhere**, ministers and top officials visit, **"but there is hardly anyone to give
> information when someone asks for specific inputs. This is the sorry state of affairs in
> tourism promotion."**

### 9.4 Old City safety & scam perceptions

⚠️ **Evidence quality is weak here.**
- Commercial travel-safety sites describe pearl fraud (fake/low-grade pearls sold as premium),
  auto overcharging, tourist-restaurant inflated pricing — but these are **low-authority sources
  with no disclosed methodology** `[SNIPPET]`
- **A systematic study of tourist safety perceptions in the Old City appears not to exist.**
  This is a genuine research gap, but it also means the perception problem is **unmeasured**.

### 9.5 Ticketing friction and language barriers
